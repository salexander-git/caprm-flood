1. COURSE PURPOSE
=================

CSCI 788 includes a required colloquium component intended to:

- help students stay on track toward project completion;
- develop presentation and visualization skills;
- develop scientific/technical writing skills;
- provide practice designing and critiquing presentations;
- support preparation of reports and posters;
- culminate in a public poster session.

The colloquium instructor oversees lectures and student presentations.

The student's overall project grade is determined through a combination of:

- faculty-advisor assessment;
- colloquium-instructor assessment;
- faculty feedback on the final poster.


2. CONFIRMED GRADE STRUCTURE
============================

The course lecture described the overall grading structure as follows:

Final report assessed by faculty advisor:
    45% of overall grade

Faculty feedback on final poster:
    15% of overall grade

Milestone deliverables assessed by faculty advisor:
    20% of overall grade

Colloquium-instructor component:
    20% of overall grade

The colloquium-instructor component was described as:

    60% presentations
    20% participation / feedback
    20% homework assignments

Expressed as approximate shares of the overall course grade:

    presentations:          12%
    participation:           4%
    homework:                4%

The 20% faculty-advisor milestone-deliverable portion is distributed across three milestones.

If divided evenly, each milestone is worth approximately:

    6.67% of the overall course grade

However, the faculty advisor is the evaluator of those milestone deliverables.


3. REQUIRED PRESENTATIONS
=========================

The course includes four class presentations:

1. Project Overview Presentation
2. Milestone 1 Presentation
3. Milestone 2 Presentation
4. Milestone 3 Presentation

The professor stated that the live presentation itself should be designed for approximately:

    8 minutes

The surrounding time slot allows additional time for questions and discussion.

The professor reasoned that an 8-minute presentation will often correspond to approximately:

    5–6 substantive slides

depending on how much time each slide requires.

This is presentation-design guidance, not a rigid slide-count rule.

The practical constraint is:

    Design the presentation to fit comfortably within 8 minutes.


4. PRESENTATION EXPECTATIONS
============================

Course presentation guidance emphasized communicating a clear technical story rather than simply listing project sections.

Presentations should generally:

- establish a compelling motivation;
- explain why the problem matters;
- use a clear storyline;
- be audience-centered;
- explain the project top-down before diving into detail;
- use concrete examples before or alongside technical detail where useful;
- avoid excessive slide density;
- use visual explanation where it improves understanding;
- make the main takeaway clear;
- present actual implementation/progress evidence;
- explain evaluation or validation rather than only describing intended future work.

The project should not spend presentation time on material that does not help the audience understand:

- the problem;
- what was built;
- what changed since the previous milestone;
- how the work was evaluated;
- what the results show;
- what remains next.

For CAPRM-Flood, milestone presentations should therefore emphasize verified progress and measured results, not merely restate the original proposal.

6. WHAT A CAPRM-FLOOD MILESTONE PRESENTATION SHOULD PROVE
=========================================================

This section is an application of the confirmed course presentation expectations to CAPRM-Flood. It is not a verbatim professor rubric.

Each milestone presentation should make it possible for the audience to understand:

1. What technical goal the milestone addressed.
2. What was actually implemented.
3. What data and methods were used.
4. What changed from the previous milestone.
5. How correctness or validity was checked.
6. What measurable results were obtained.
7. What limitations or unresolved issues remain, where relevant.
8. What the next milestone will do.

For an 8-minute presentation, only the strongest evidence should be shown.

Do not attempt to present the entire repository.

15. CANONICAL SUMMARY
=====================

CSCI 788 requires three milestone deliverables assessed by the faculty advisor and three corresponding milestone presentations in the colloquium, in addition to the initial project overview presentation. Presentations should be designed for approximately eight minutes, with additional time reserved for questions/discussion; roughly five to six substantive slides is reasonable guidance rather than a hard rule. The course emphasizes clear, audience-centered technical communication, concrete progress, evaluation, scientific/technical writing, and final poster preparation. The final report carries 45% of the overall grade, faculty poster feedback 15%, faculty-advisor milestone deliverables 20%, and the colloquium-instructor component 20%.
